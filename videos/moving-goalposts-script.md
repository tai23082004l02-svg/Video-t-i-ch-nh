# Moving the Goalposts — When enough keeps changing.

If you do not recognize this manipulation tactic, you can spend years chasing approval that never comes.

Not because you never do enough. Because enough keeps changing.

It starts with a condition that sounds reasonable.

They say, explain what happened, and we can move on.

You explain. Now an explanation is not enough. They want proof.

You provide proof. Now they question your motives.

Every time you meet the requirement, a new one takes its place.

This is called moving the goalposts.

You think you are resolving a disagreement. But the finish line moves every time you approach it.

The trap is what happens next. You start working harder instead of questioning the rules.

More explaining. More proving. Less confidence in your own judgment.

But a changed request is not automatically manipulation.

New information can justify a new standard. People can misunderstand each other.

Look for the repeated pattern: clear agreements get replaced, and your effort is never acknowledged.

Before you defend yourself again, ask one specific question.

What exactly would resolve this, and will that still count once I do it?

Put the agreement into clear words. If the standard changes, name the change.

You can say: I met the condition we agreed on. I am willing to discuss new concerns separately.

You do not have to win an endless argument to deserve fair treatment.

Sometimes the next step is not a better explanation. It is a clearer boundary.
